# Learning-Pathway Review — Market Architects as a Business Curriculum

**Reviewer role:** Learning designer / curriculum analyst
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Method:** Evaluated the site (574 pages: 9 models, 4 pathways, 8 playbooks, 63 leaders, 66 lectures, 56 books) against what a working adult needs from a self-taught "poor man's business course." Assessed for learning progression, prerequisite logic, completeness of the business lifecycle, and the presence of an assessment/application loop.

---

## Executive summary

The site is an outstanding **reference library** and a solid **inspiration engine** — but as a **curriculum** it has three structural gaps:

1. **No progression.** The 4 pathways are thematic sequences (founder-journey, ideas-explorer), not a staged curriculum. A learner can't tell what to study first, or which ideas unlock which. There's no "Level 1 → Level 5."
2. **Front-loaded toward market strategy, missing the back half of the business lifecycle.** The taxonomy and content are heavy on *attacking the market* (zero-to-one, distribution, pricing) and light on *operating* it: **quality/manufacturing** (Deming, lean/TPS, zero-defects) is entirely absent, and **employee leadership/incentives** (how to motivate a team once you have headcount) is under-served.
3. **No application checkpoint.** There's "Apply This Model" per lecture and problem-based playbooks, but no staged exercises, no "you've finished a level" moments, and no curriculum-level synthesis.

The fix this round: **an organic skill tree** — a single self-guided course that sequences the library into tiers (Think → Create → Enter → Operate → Lead → Endure), with quality and employee-leadership nodes filling the lifecycle gap. This turns the buffet into a degree.

## Finding 1: The lifecycle is incomplete — the "back half" is missing [HIGH]

A business lifecycle has five phases. Current coverage:

| Lifecycle phase | Coverage today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Think** (mental models, decision science) | Model 09, strong | ✅ Good |
| **2. Create** (product, zero-to-one) | Models 01–02, strong | ✅ Good |
| **3. Enter & Spread** (distribution, pricing, bootstrapping) | Models 04–05, strong | ✅ Good |
| **4. Operate** (moats, cash flow, **quality, manufacturing**) | Model 03 + playbooks; **no quality/manufacturing canon** | ⚠️ **Gap — the user's exact request** |
| **5. Lead & Reward** (purpose, teams, **employee incentives**) | Model 07 (purpose/trust); **no incentive/motivation theory** | ⚠️ **Partial gap** |

The user's phrasing — *"beyond analyzing and attacking the market… once you have penetration you want leadership to incentivize employees and manufacturing quality control to ensure perfect quality"* — is a precise diagnosis of the missing back half.

**Fix (implemented this round):**
- New model **10 — Quality & Manufacturing Excellence**: Deming (statistical process control, 14 points), Ohno (Toyota Production System, 7 wastes), Shingo (Zero Quality Control, poka-yoke), Goldratt (Theory of Constraints).
- Employee-incentive thinkers added to model 07 — Purpose & Leadership: McGregor (Theory X/Y), Herzberg (motivation-hygiene), Dan Pink (Drive: autonomy/mastery/purpose).
- These fill the "operate well" and "lead & reward" phases.

## Finding 2: No curriculum progression — prerequisites and levels don't exist [HIGH]

Today the strongest entry point is the two-door "pathways" page, but the 4 pathways are parallel tours, not a staged syllabus. A learner cannot answer: *"What's the order? What builds on what? How do I know I'm done with the basics?"*

**Fix (implemented this round):** an **organic skill tree** — a single course in six tiers:

```
Tier 0  Think First      → mental models, decision science, focus   (prerequisites)
Tier 1  Create           → zero-to-one, product intuition
Tier 2  Enter & Spread   → distribution, bootstrapping, persuasion
Tier 3  Operate          → moats + QUALITY & MANUFACTURING          (NEW)
Tier 4  Lead & Reward    → purpose + EMPLOYEE INCENTIVES            (NEW)
Tier 5  Endure & Renew   → resilience, pivots, paradigm shifts
```

Each node is a real lecture/book in the library, with a one-line "unlocks the next" rationale. The tree is deliberately a **progression, not a menu**: you earn your way to Tier 3 by having distribution, and to Tier 4 by having a team.

## Finding 3: No application loop at curriculum level [MEDIUM]

Per-lecture "Apply This Model" and per-problem playbooks exist, but there is no curriculum-level checkpoint. The skill tree adds light completion tracking (mark-a-node-done, course progress bar) so a learner can see the course advance — the "am I actually doing this" feedback the current pathways lack.

**Recommended (future):** a one-page worksheet per tier (e.g., "Tier 3 Operate worksheet: document one process with a poka-yoke") that converts the tree from reading into doing.

## What's already working as a pathway

- **Per-lecture pedagogy is excellent**: 3–5 min annotated timestamps, Apply This Model, cross-references, transcripts — each node is a complete micro-lesson.
- **Problem-based routing (playbooks) is genuinely SMB-native** and correctly fills the "I have a problem" entry.
- **Cross-referencing** (complementary/opposing takes) creates the "dialogue between experts" that makes self-study feel like a seminar.
- **The two-door entry** is the right onboarding metaphor and can stay — the skill tree is the destination it points to.

## Recommended actions (this round and next)

1. ✅ Add the quality/manufacturing + employee-incentive content (Finding 1).
2. ✅ Ship the skill tree as the primary learning path (Finding 2).
3. ⏳ Per-tier worksheets ("Tier 3: document a process and add one mistake-proofing step") — the application loop (Finding 3).
4. ⏳ A "learning path" indicator that maps the skill tree tiers onto the two entry doors on the home page.
